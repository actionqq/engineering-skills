# Engineering Document Archival

Keep current guidance easy to find and historical material traceable. Communicate in the user's language. A milestone, completed task, old date, or large directory can prompt assessment; none makes a document automatically archivable. Archival is not a mandatory end-of-task step.

## Establish the requested operation

Distinguish assessment, execution, historical lookup, and restoration from the request. An assessment produces a concrete disposition list without editing its subjects. An explicit archive or restore request authorizes the necessary bounded organization work; do not add a per-file confirmation ritual. Ask only about a material unresolved choice, and continue independent authorized items.

Inspect the project's document roles, archive conventions, indexes, repository state, and requested scope. Start with an inventory and candidate clusters, then read the candidates and their relevant references; do not treat filenames as evidence of contents. Report the examined scope instead of claiming a whole-repository audit after sampling.

For assessment or archiving, read [Disposition and authority](references/assessment.md). Before changing files or indexes, read [Execution and recovery](references/operations.md). For historical lookup, archive visibility, or restoration, read [Retrieval and restoration](references/retrieval.md).

## Decide what may leave active use

Keep work outcome, document authority, and archival disposition separate. Implemented work may still need its design as current guidance. A cancelled proposal may be archived without passing tests. A paused plan may still be active. A newer draft does not supersede an accepted decision simply because it is newer.

Choose a disposition per document or coherent group: retain, carry forward information before archiving, move to archive, archive in place, preserve a snapshot, or defer for a named uncertainty. Explain the evidence, successor or maintained owner where applicable, affected references, and destination. A batch is an execution convenience, not proof that every file has the same lifecycle.

Preserve any unique current contract or unresolved obligation before retiring its only accessible source. Carry forward established information within scope; do not accept a proposal, invent verification, or resolve a disputed design merely to make archival possible. Partial supersession requires a claim-level assessment, not a whole-document historical label.

## Execute and make the result discoverable

Follow existing layout and stable identities. Choose physical movement, an index/status change, or a historical snapshot according to readers, links, and whether the source continues evolving. Do not impose a versioned workflow, rename every file, or create a second source of current truth.

Preserve original historical content and useful evidence. Update affected links, lifecycle notes, and navigation together; identify the current starting point and the historical starting point. An archive summary is a pointer, not a replacement for original material. Historical corrections should be dated and distinguishable from the original account.

Use a recoverable mapping and inspect actual outcomes. Do not overwrite a collision, silently discard untracked files, or claim a batch is atomic. On interruption, reconcile source, destination, and indexes before retrying. Archival does not authorize deletion, commits, tags, publication, changes to global memory, or unrelated project-policy edits.

## Completion

For assessment, deliver the disposition list and remaining uncertainties. For execution, verify preservation, destinations, affected links and anchors, navigation, and visibility conventions. Report completed, unchanged, deferred, and failed items with a usable archive entrypoint and recovery information proportional to the change.

For restoration, verify both placement and applicability; restored content does not automatically regain decision authority or fresh verification. Structural checks prove organization, not implementation or runtime acceptance. No candidates is a valid result. Ordinary implementation continues to own its documentation updates; this workflow organizes accumulated material rather than postponing all maintenance until archival.
