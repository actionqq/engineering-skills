# Review Reports

Use this to format findings that a review has already established. It does not perform the review, invent defects, upgrade evidence, or change the review's readiness conclusion.

## Preserve the source review

Follow the repository's review format when one exists. Keep materially different review axes separate when the supplied findings distinguish them. Do not force every review into a universal issue/impact/recommendation template.

A cold reader should be able to identify the reviewed target and snapshot, the requirements or standards used, validation that actually ran, material evidence limits, findings, and the supplied conclusion when one exists.

Preserve the review's evidence meanings and uncertainty exactly. `Not verified` or an equivalent missing-evidence state is not a softer pass; formatting does not authorize changing it to verified. Do not invent a new check merely to fill a section.

## Keep each supplied finding actionable

Retain the location or target, trigger or scenario, evidence or violated requirement, impact, and correction direction that the review established. These are information requirements, not mandatory headings. Group duplicate symptoms only when doing so preserves the original meaning and severity.

When the supplied review has no findings, keep its scope, validation, and limits rather than inflating the report with generic advice. For multi-review material, preserve stated independence limits and synthesize already-established duplicate findings without treating votes as proof.

Use concrete project vocabulary and current locations. The finished report should be readable without the reviewer conversation while remaining faithful to the review that produced it.
